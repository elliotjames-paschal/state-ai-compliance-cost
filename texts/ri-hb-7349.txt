40.1-5.5-1. Short title.  7 
This chapter shall be known and may be cited as the "Oversight of Artificial Intell igence 8 
Technology in Mental Health Care Act". 9 
40.1-5.5-2. Definitions.  10 
For the purposes of this chapter: 11 
(1) "Administrative support" means tasks performed to assist a licensed professional or 12 
provider in the delivery of therapy or psychotherapy services that do not involve therapeutic 13 
communication. "Administrative support" includes, but is not limited to, the following: 14 
(i) Managing appointment scheduling and reminders; 15 
(ii) Processing billing and insurance claims; and 16 
(iii) Drafting general communicatio ns related to therapy logistics that do not include 17 
therapeutic advice. 18

(2) "Artificial intelligence", "artificial intelligence technology", or "AI" means a machine-1 
based system that can, for a given set of human -defined objectives, make predictions, 2 
recommendations, or decisions influencing real or virtual environments. 3 
(3) "Consent" means an affirmative written agreement, including by electronic means, 4 
which unambiguously communicates the individual's explicit, express, freely given, informed, 5 
voluntary, and specific agreement, and which is revocable by the individual. "Consent" does not 6 
include an agreement that is obtained by the following: 7 
(i) The acceptance of a general or broad terms of use, agreement, or a similar document 8 
that contains descriptions of artificial intelligence along with other unrelated information; 9 
(ii) An individual hovering over, muting, pausing, or closing a given piece of digital 10 
content; or 11 
(iii) Using deceptive actions. 12 
(4) "Licensed professional or provider" means an individual  who holds a valid license, 13 
credential, or certification issued by this state to provide therapy or psychotherapy services. 14 
(5) "Peer support" means services provided by individuals with lived experience of mental 15 
health conditions or recovery from substan ce use or gambling that are intended to offer 16 
encouragement, understanding, and guidance without clinical intervention. 17 
(6) "Permitted use of artificial intelligence" means the use of artificial intelligence tools or 18 
systems by a licensed professional or p rovider to assist in providing administrative support or 19 
supplementary support in therapy or psychotherapy services where the licensed professional or 20 
provider maintains responsibility for clinical judgement and reasonable therapeutic oversight of the 21 
patient's use of the system, but not for vendor-controlled system design, algorithms, or outputs. 22 
(7) "Religious counseling" means counseling provided by clergy members, pastoral 23

are explicitly faith-based and are not represented as clinical mental health services or therapy or 25 
psychotherapy services. 26 
(8) "Supplementary support" means tasks performed to assist a licensed professional or 27 
provider in the deliv ery of therapy or psychotherapy services that do not involve therapeutic 28 
communication and that are not administrative support. "Supplementary support" includes, but is 29 
not limited to, the following: 30 
(i) Preparing and maintaining client records, including therapy notes; 31 
(ii) Analyzing data to track client progress or identify trends, subject to review by a 32 
licensed, credentialed, or certified professional; and 33 
(iii) Identifying and organizing external resources or referrals for client use. 34

40.1-5.5-3. Artificial intelligence in mental health care: use restrictions and 18 
permissions.  19 
(a) No licensed professional or provider shall be permitted to use artificial intelligence, 20 
designed to simulate emotional a ttachment, bonding, or dependency or artificial intelligence 21 
companions for mental health or emotional support, to assist in supplementary support or 22 
therapeutic communication in  therapy or psychotherapy services where the client's therapeutic 23 
session is recorded or transcribed unless the patient or the patient's parent, guardian or other legally 24 
authorized representative is informed in writing of the following and provides consen t as defined 25 
in § 40.1-5.5-2: 26 
(1) That artificial intelligence will be used;  27 
(2) The specific purpose of the artificial intelligence tool or system that will be used; and 28 
(3) The patient or the patient's parents, or other legally authorized representative, provides 29 
consent to the use of artificial intelligence. 30 
(b) An individual, corporation, or entity may not provide, advertise, or otherwise offer 31 
therapy or psychotherapy services, including through the use of Internet -based artificial 32 
intelligence, to the public in this state unless the therapy or psychotherapy services are conducted 33 
by an individual who is a licensed professional or provider. 34

(c) A licensed professional or provider may use artificial intelligence only to the extent that 1 
such use meets th e requirements of subsection (a) of this section. A licensed professional or 2 
provider may not allow or otherwise use artificial intelligence to do any of the following: 3 
(1) Make independent therapeutic decisions; 4 
(2) Directly interact with clients in any f orm of therapeutic communication without an 5 
established relationship when the licensed professional or provider agrees to undertake diagnosis 6 
and treatment of the patient and the patient consents under this section, whether  or not there has 7 
been an in -person encounter between the licensed professional or provider and the patient. The 8 
provider retains responsibility for clinical judgement and reasonable therapeutic oversight of the 9 
patient's use of the system, but not for vendor-controlled system design, algorithms, or outputs; or 10 
(3) Determine therapeutic recommendations or treatment plans. 11 
(d) If a client discloses that they are using software that includes AI features on their own 12 
initiative, the licensed professional or provider may discuss the client's use of the software and offer 13 
guidance about the client's use of the software. The licensed professional is responsible for 14 
maintaining confidentiality and monitoring the safety of the client, intervening when necessary to 15 
protect the safety of the client, and to discuss limitations and risks using the software poses to the 16 
patient. 17 
40.1-5.5-4. Confidentiality of records.  18 
All records kept by a licensed professional or provider and all communications between an 19 
individual seeking therapy or psychotherapy services and a licensed professional or provider shall 20 
be confidential and shall not be disclosed except as provided pursuant to the provisions of § 40.1 -21 
5-26. 22 
40.1-5.5-5. Penalties.  23 
(a) The penalties pursuant to § 5 -37.3-9 shall apply to any violation of  the confidentiality 24 
provisions of this chapter. 25 
(b) The executive office of health and human services shall have authority to investigate 26 
any actual, alleged, or suspected violation of this chapter. 27 
(c) This chapter shall not apply to the following: 28 
(1) Religious counseling; 29 
(2) Peer support; and 30 
(3) Self-help materials and educational resources that are available to the public and do not 31 
purport to offer therapy or psychotherapy services. 32 
(4) Any artificial intelligence tool or system that has been reviewed and cleared for use by 33 
the federal Food and Drug Administration, or another federal agency tasked with approving 34
