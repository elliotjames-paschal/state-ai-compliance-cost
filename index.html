Method in brief

  • Bottom-up. Each law is coded into the duties it demands; each duty carries hour estimates for the average AI model. Cost is hours × rates — the same approach federal agencies use for regulatory burden estimates.
  • Increment, not total. The hypothetical federal standard is each family’s least common denominator — the parameters every state sets identically. That shared core is never claimed; only divergence beyond it is.
  • Reuse from statutes. Within a family, each additional state costs (1 − reuse) of a fresh build. Reuse is derived live from the coded parameters — the share two states set to the same value — never assigned.
  • Range, not point. 4,000 seeded Monte Carlo draws over duty hours, with a shared estimation-error factor across bills. The conservative end leads.

Every input is auditable: the coded dataset with quoted statutory sources ships in the repo (data/bills.js, full records in coding/).

How to read this model

What the terms mean and how the pieces fit together — duties, parameters, reuse, and the federal baseline. Every value described here is auditable in the repo.

Glossary reference for every term, duty, and parameter in the model

Core concepts

TermDefinition
CategoryWho a law binds: company (enters the estimate), criminal (binds individuals), public-sector (binds the state), education (binds schools). Only company laws are counted by default.
Requirement familyA group of laws that demand the same kind of thing across states (chatbot safeguards, insurance decisions, …). Reuse and the federal baseline are computed within a family.
DutyA unit of work a law demands (build a crisis protocol, run an annual audit). Each duty carries hour estimates — legal and engineering once, operations per year. Duties drive cost.
ParameterA concrete setting a statute picks (age limit 18, notice in 90 days). Parameters never add cost directly; they measure how much states agree. Every coded value is anchored to a quoted provision.
ReuseThe share of parameters two states set to the same value, averaged across state pairs in a family. Each later state pays only (1 − reuse) of a fresh build.
Federal baselineThe parameters every state in a family sets identically — the least common denominator. A single federal rule would require that work anyway, so it is never counted. Its size is shown under the headline.
Statutory exposureThe penalty a law itself states for violations (amount, unit, enforcer, cure period). Reported as fact next to each bill; never added to the cost estimate.
Time horizonHow many years of recurring (operations) costs are counted. One-time costs are unaffected.
Monte CarloThe model runs 4,000 seeded simulations with duty hours drawn from their ranges, plus a shared error factor across all draws. Results are the spread: conservative p10, central p50, upper p90.

How a law becomes a number

  1. Every enacted state AI law was read and coded into four facts: who it binds, its requirement family, the duties it demands, and the parameters it sets — each anchored to a quoted provision.
  2. Duties → cost. A law's cost is the sum of its duties' hours × your rates (legal and engineering once; operations each year of the horizon).
  3. Parameters → discounts. Parameter agreement between states sets each family's reuse; what all states agree on is the federal baseline, which is never counted.
  4. Within each family: the first state pays its divergence from the federal baseline; every later state pays (1 − reuse) of a fresh build plus its recurring operations.
  5. 4,000 seeded simulation runs turn the hour ranges into the reported spread — conservative end first.

Duties reference

Default hours are for the average AI model and editable under Advanced on the Cost Model tab. Format: legal / engineering (one-time) · operations (per year).
